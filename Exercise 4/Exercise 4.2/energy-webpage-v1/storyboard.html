How Much Energy Does Your TV Use?

A simple look at how television display technology and screen size may influence annual energy consumption.

Created for young adults who want to better understand the technology they use every day.

QUESTION

Do Newer TV Display Technologies Use Less Energy?

Newer television technologies are often associated with improved performance and efficiency. But does a newer display technology actually mean lower annual energy consumption?

This visualisation compares the average labelled annual energy consumption of LCD, LCD (LED) and OLED televisions in the dataset.

KEY TAKEAWAY

In this dataset, newer display technology does not mean lower annual energy consumption.

LCD televisions recorded the lowest average annual energy consumption. LCD (LED) televisions were higher, while OLED televisions recorded the highest average among the three display technologies shown.

This suggests that newer display technology alone should not be treated as a guarantee of lower energy use.

QUESTION

Do Bigger TVs Use More Energy?

Bigger screens may offer a more immersive viewing experience, but does choosing a larger television also mean using more energy?

This scatter plot compares television screen size with labelled annual energy consumption. Each point represents a television in the dataset.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Larger TVs generally use more energy.

The scatter plot shows a clear upward pattern. As screen size increases, labelled annual energy consumption generally rises.

Larger screen sizes also show a wider spread of energy consumption. This means that televisions of a similar size can still differ considerably in how much energy they use.

For buyers, screen size matters, but it should not be the only factor considered when comparing the energy efficiency of different televisions.
